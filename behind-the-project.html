Behind the project

Bridging the gap between theoretical knowledge and clinical practice.

Why was this resource created?

Limited exposure to vestibular rehabilitation during clinical placements may leave physiotherapy students feeling underprepared to assess and manage patients presenting with dizziness. This resource was created to bridge the gap between theory and practice, allowing students to develop their clinical reasoning skills in a safe and interactive learning environment.

From research to resource

Development began with research into vestibular assessment and management using peer-reviewed literature, clinical practice guidelines and university teaching materials. This information was used to develop six clinical cases covering BPPV, vestibular hypofunction and central causes of dizziness. Beginner and advanced cases were created to support different levels of knowledge and clinical experience.

Bringing the concept to life

An interactive website was designed to make the clinical content engaging and accessible. AI was used as a learning tool to develop coding skills, which were then applied in Visual Studio Code to build the website. AI also supported troubleshooting and problem-solving throughout development. Ongoing testing and refinement helped improve the website's design, navigation and interactive features.

Evaluation and refinement

The resource was shared with expert stakeholders, whose feedback helped guide improvements. The Learning Object Review Instrument (LORI) and a student feedback questionnaire were selected to assess the quality of the resource, how easy it is to use and how it supports student learning. This feedback will help guide future improvements.
